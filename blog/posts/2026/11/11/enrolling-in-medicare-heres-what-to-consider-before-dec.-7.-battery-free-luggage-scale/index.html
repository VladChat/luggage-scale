Medicare Open Enrollment Guide for Travelers

The postcard sits under the fridge magnet: a turquoise cove, a strip of sugar-white sand, a trim sailboat pinned to the horizon. It’s the place Linda and Carlos have circled on the map for years. They’re 67 now—finally unhurried, finally free to travel off-season. But there’s a date they can’t ignore. December 7. It stares at them from a sticky note by the coffee maker, a reminder that the right choice in health coverage could make those winter flights effortless—or complicated.

They’ve learned a few lessons already. Last spring, Linda’s knee flared up in Santa Fe. The clinic took her insurance, but the bill had surprises. The prescription her doctor preferred wasn’t on the plan’s list. Carlos spent an hour on hold to confirm coverage while the sun slid behind the adobe roofs. They still loved the trip, but it taught them something vital: adventure rewards the prepared.

Which is why they’re here at the kitchen table again. The window is cracked. Chill air carries the scent of wet leaves. A pencil ticks through questions: Will our doctors still be in network? What happens if we get sick in another state? Can we see a specialist without months of delay? Will our meds still be covered—and at what cost?

On the stove, the kettle starts to twitch. Carlos grabs it and pours two mugs. “Let’s get this done,” he says, half smiling. Linda nods. She still wants the cove. The boat. The freedom of a carry-on that slides like a whisper into the overhead bin. She also wants to know that if life happens—because it always does—they won’t spend a day of vacation glued to a customer service line, or worse, postponing care.

Open enrollment doesn’t shout. It sits quietly on the calendar while the holidays crowd in and the year tilts toward dark. But those weeks between mid-October and early December carry weight. For travelers like Linda and Carlos, choosing well means more than premiums and brochures. It means fitting your coverage to the way you live now: on the move, curious, unwilling to stay put.

And it starts with a clear plan—before the deadline slips by.

The clock to Dec. 7

The calendar looks roomy in October. Then daylight shrinks, errands multiply, and it’s December before you can say “boarding group.” Don’t let the last week force a quick choice.

Key points to lock in now:

  • Know the dates. Open enrollment runs from October 15 through December 7.
  • Identify your starting point. Your current plan will auto-renew if you do nothing, even if its costs rise.
  • Look for your Annual Notice of Change (ANOC). That letter explains next year’s premiums, copays, and drug list changes.

Why shop if you’re satisfied? Because plans change. Formularies update. Networks shift. If you travel, out-of-area support can make or break your winter plans.

According to a CBS report, this window is your chance to compare options side by side and fix mismatches before the new year begins. Give yourself at least an hour. You’ll save far more time later.

What you can change

The options are straightforward, but their implications aren’t. In this window, you can:

  • Switch between Original Medicare and a Medicare Advantage plan.
  • Join, drop, or change a Part D prescription drug plan.
  • Move from one Medicare Advantage plan to another.

A quick refresher:

  • Original Medicare (Parts A and B) covers hospital and outpatient care. Many people add a standalone Part D plan for prescriptions and may buy a Medigap policy to help with deductibles and coinsurance.
  • Medicare Advantage (Part C) bundles A and B, often D, and may fold in extras like dental, vision, fitness, and some travel-friendly benefits. It also sets an annual out-of-pocket maximum, which Original Medicare doesn’t include by itself.

Trade-offs matter. Advantage plans can be cost-effective but usually require you to stay in-network for non-emergencies. Original Medicare paired with Medigap can offer broader provider choice, which frequent travelers sometimes prefer—but total monthly costs may be higher.

What to look for this year:

  • Did premiums or copays change?
  • Are your doctors still in network?
  • Are your meds still covered at a preferred cost?
  • Is there better telehealth access or urgent-care flexibility?
  • For Advantage plans, what is the annual out-of-pocket maximum?

Compare through a traveler’s lens

Let’s be honest: Coverage that fits a homebody might frustrate a traveler. Evaluate like you plan to use it—in motion.

H3: Out-of-state care If you visit family across the country, confirm how non-emergency visits work when you’re away from home. Some plans maintain national networks or offer guest membership programs. Others may only cover out-of-network care at higher costs, or limit you to urgent and emergency services.

H3: International trips Original Medicare generally doesn’t cover care outside the U.S., with narrow exceptions. Some Medigap plans provide limited foreign travel emergency coverage, often with caps and deductibles. Select Medicare Advantage plans include emergency or urgent care abroad, but you must read the fine print. If your winter plans include another country, price a separate travel medical policy. Budget it like a flight; it’s part of the trip.

H3: Telehealth access When the hotel Wi-Fi cooperates and your throat feels scratchy, a video visit can save the day. Confirm:

  • Which telehealth platforms your plan supports.
  • Whether urgent care and behavioral health are included.
  • Costs for virtual visits versus in-person care.

H3: Timely specialist access Ask how referrals work, and how long it typically takes to see a specialist. If a plan needs referrals for most services, know the steps. Vacation isn’t the place to learn a process under pressure.

H3: Vaccines and preventive care Seasonal vaccines matter when you travel in winter. Check coverage for flu and COVID boosters at retail pharmacies on the road. Preventive visits and screenings should be easy to schedule before you go.

Drug coverage and preventive care

Medication costs can swing a plan from “okay” to “ouch.” Before you decide, run your list through a plan finder tool and confirm:

  • Each drug’s tier and copay at preferred versus standard pharmacies.
  • Which pharmacies are “preferred” where you travel.
  • Any prior authorization or step therapy requirements.

If a plan pushes your medication to a higher-cost tier, ask your doctor whether a clinically equivalent alternative fits your situation. Don’t accept sticker shock without a conversation.

Consider a three-month supply

  • Request 90-day refills for maintenance meds before your trip.
  • Use mail-order only if delivery timing is reliable and secure.
  • Keep meds in original containers in your carry-on, not checked bags.

Preventive care strategy

  • Schedule an annual wellness visit before your departure. Clarify next steps for labs or imaging.
  • Get winter vaccines at least two weeks before travel.
  • Print or save your current medication list, allergies, and plan ID to your phone’s wallet app.

A simple rule: Smooth drug coverage equals smoother travel. Even a single uncovered prescription can derail a day, if not a weekend.

Networks, costs, and fine print

Good coverage is more than a low premium. For travelers, it’s about how a plan behaves when you’re away from your ZIP code.

What to examine closely:

  1. Network design
  • Are urgent-care centers covered out of area?
  • Do walk-in clinics at national pharmacy chains count as in-network?
  • Are your specialists available in multiple metro areas?
  1. Annual out-of-pocket ceiling
  • For Advantage plans, find the maximum out-of-pocket (MOOP). This is your worst-case scenario in a tough health year. If you’re risk-averse, a lower MOOP can buy peace of mind.
  1. Prior authorization
  • Learn which services require approval. Imaging? Physical therapy? Specialty drugs?
  • Ask how long approvals take and whether out-of-area providers can submit digitally.
  1. Extras that matter to travelers
  • Routine dental and vision, if included, should be usable beyond your home region.
  • Fitness benefits that work at national gym chains or through digital apps.
  • Transportation support for medical visits in unfamiliar cities.
  1. Customer service reality
  • Call the plan with a specific scenario. Note hold times and clarity of answers.
  • Use the website or app. If it’s clunky at home, it’ll be worse in a hotel lobby.

Three steps to decide in 45 minutes

  • Step 1 (15 minutes): List your providers, meds, and typical services. Rank what matters most: low premium, wide network, specialist access, or prescription savings.
  • Step 2 (20 minutes): Compare two to three plans side by side. Plug your meds into the plan finder. Check the MOOP. Confirm networks in the cities you visit.
  • Step 3 (10 minutes): Call a plan’s helpline with one targeted question, like “How do you handle urgent care in Phoenix for members based in Boston?” Trust what you experience.

Actionable tips

  • Screenshot your plan ID, digital pharmacy card, and customer service numbers.
  • Star your preferred urgent-care locations on Google Maps in your travel cities.
  • Save a copy of your ANOC letter; it’s your next-year playbook.
  • Keep a steady monthly health budget. Include premiums, average copays, and one “surprise visit.”
  • If you split the year between places, confirm how the plan treats both addresses.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Can I switch from Medicare Advantage to Original Medicare during open enrollment? A: Yes. From October 15 to December 7, you can move between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage. If you choose Original Medicare, consider adding a Part D plan for prescriptions and explore Medigap options, noting that Medigap enrollment rules vary by state and timing.

Q: I travel often. Which type of plan usually offers more nationwide flexibility? A: Many frequent travelers prefer Original Medicare with a suitable Medigap policy for broad provider access. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer strong national networks or guest memberships. Check your specific doctors, urgent-care options, and out-of-area rules before deciding.

Q: Does Medicare cover medical care outside the United States? A: Original Medicare generally does not, with limited exceptions. Some Medigap plans include capped foreign travel emergency benefits, and certain Medicare Advantage plans offer emergency/urgent coverage abroad. Verify details and consider a separate travel medical policy for international trips.

Q: How do I make sure my prescriptions are still affordable next year? A: Use a plan finder tool to confirm each medication’s tier, copay, and preferred pharmacy status. Compare at least two plans, check for prior authorization requirements, and ask your doctor about clinically appropriate alternatives if a drug moves to a higher tier.

Q: What small gear helps reduce travel stress when managing health needs? A: Keep a slim document pouch for cards and receipts, a clear pill organizer, a reliable phone charger, basic first-aid items, and a battery free luggage scale to avoid last-minute repacking or overweight fees. These low-maintenance tools support smoother, more focused trips.
