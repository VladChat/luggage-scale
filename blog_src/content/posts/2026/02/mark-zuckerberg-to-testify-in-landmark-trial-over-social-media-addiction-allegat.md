## Designing a Calmer Journey

Think of this as preflight control. Small choices, compounded, yield a steadier mind on the move.

### 1) Set the phone up for travel

- Prune your home screen. Keep only travel‑critical apps visible: airline, maps, transit, notes, camera.
- Turn off badges and banners for everything else. Alarms and calls? Sure. A hundred dopamine pings? Pass.
- Add a Focus/Do Not Disturb schedule that activates from departure to arrival.

### 2) Prepare your info offline

- Download boarding passes and transit tickets to your wallet app.
- Save offline maps for your destination and any connection city.
- Capture hotel confirmations, addresses, and emergency contacts as PDFs or notes.

### 3) Tame the infinite scroll

- Move social apps to a “Later” folder on the last screen.
- Add 10‑minute timers. If you break them, own it and reset—zero shame, just awareness.
- Decide windows for check‑ins: maybe at breakfast and after dinner, not in security lines.

### 4) Build a low‑tech buffer

Digital discipline is easier when analog options are ready.

- Paperback or e‑reader with airplane mode on.
- Slim notebook for lists, thoughts, and busking out directions.
- Pen that writes upside down on bumpy trays.
- Small, reliable tools that don’t need charging when you least expect it.

Actionable tip: Write a one‑page “trip script” the night before. Bullet the day’s moves: wake time, commute, check‑in, gate, snack, landing. When you’re foggy, read the script, not the feed.

## Build an Analog Packing Ritual

Packing marks the line between intention and inertia. Make it tactile.

- Lay everything on a bed or table. Touch each item. Name its job. If it has no job, it stays home.
- Print the addresses and phone numbers you must not lose. Fold that sheet into your passport cover.
- Stash a paper copy of your travel insurance and a photo of your ID in a separate pocket.
- Add a pocket guide or city map. Yes, your phone can do it all—until it can’t.

A simple checklist pays for itself when your brain is oatmeal at dawn:

1) Wallet, passport, printed itinerary.
2) Medication for two extra days, not just the exact count.
3) One book you actually want to finish.
4) Light jacket that doubles as a pillow.
5) A compact, sturdy tool for bag prep before check-in.

Notice how none of these depend on a full battery. They’re quiet, sure-handed, and ready when the charging station has a queue like a theme park coaster. They also create tiny islands of attention—little refuges from the stream where you can catch your breath, regroup, and keep moving.
